When we look up at a clear night sky, we are unknowingly engaging in the ultimate act of time travel. The light radiating from distant stars journeys through the vacuum of space at an astonishing speed of roughly 299,792 kilometers per second, yet the universe is so unimaginably vast that this glow can take thousands, or even millions, of years to reach our eyes on Earth. Consequently, the twinkling constellations we admire today are merely radiant "ghosts" of the past; some of those celestial bodies may have already collapsed or burned out long before human civilization even began. It is a profoundly humbling reality that simply by tilting our heads upward, we are directly connecting with the ancient, enduring history of the cosmos.
